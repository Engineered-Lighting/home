---
title: The den spot is directed, not switched
target: backend
type: changed
---

The den spotlight preview (`tools/den-live/preview.py`) now plays every change of its light decision the way a director of photography would: it uses the same FC13 commands a real C12 fixture executes, and Home can draw the same motion. The rules for when a spot lights are unchanged. The new `direction.py` decides how each change looks.
- **It blooms on.** A new task light rises over 2 s on a quarter-sine curve: visible with the yes itself, and half way at a third of its time. The fade curves are the C12's own (ease_out, ease_in_out, linear, as `c12.move` names them), so a fade played as a train is the one a board with the timed MOVE plays from a single command. The board has no fade, so the rise is a level train: one stationary MOVE each time the 0–254 level changes. A virtual fixture sends at most one a 0.1 s tick. A real one sends at most one every 0.5 s, the firmware's 2-commands-a-second rule; the steps renew the lease themselves, so no keep-lit goes between them. The light Home shows is always the last MOVE the trace committed.
- **One MOVE per cue on a board that can fade.** Where `c12.get_profile` advertises the timed MOVE (firmware v2-b and later) and the codec can encode it:
  - each fade is one MOVE carrying `fade_ms` and its curve;
  - a dark move followed by a rise is one MOVE that blooms on arrival;
  - an exit during a glide joins the flight;
  - a lit glide takes a 1 s floor (`glide_min_s`): the same S-curve, slower.

  The published light follows the board's own fade. Virtual fixtures and boards without it, such as Light 1 on v2-a, keep the capped level train.
- **It glides once.** The aim is the page, not the wrists. A book is read square to the line of sight and held by its lower half, so the aim is 5 cm up the page from the hands (`page_up_m`): ahead of and above them at the chest, ahead of them in the lap, never back toward the face. A settled median replaces den-core's first-sample shift filter. A page turn never moves a lit spot. A real shift moves it once, as one lit MOVE, after the book comes to rest. Each glide is published with the S-curve the board flies (`glide`: from, to, start, duration and path limits). The S-curve is the firmware's own (its duration.py at 62ea043, with a separate deceleration limit and the timed MOVE's exact time-stretch), checked against all 36 of its planner vectors.
- **It doesn't flip-flop.** A seated reader's facing is averaged over 3 s, so a side-on fixture isn't flipped in and out of "in front of you" by the shoulders' frame-to-frame swing. A lit fixture keeps its job while its beam's centre clears the head by 12 cm (admission still needs 15 cm), and while it's no more than 5° in front of side-on, den-core's own margin and the owner's setting (`facing_keep_deg`, which the owner may set up to 10°; admission is den-core's side-on either way). A fixture already pre-aimed keeps the pre-aim. While a spot is lit, the runner-up waits on the same page, dark, so a hand-off is a 1.2 s crossfade and never a dark move. A crossfade keeps the page's light whole: den-core's dimming curve gives 18% of the light at level 0.5, so two ramps eased in levels would leave the page with 37% half way. A level train eases the light itself. A board with the timed MOVE uses its own curves, one MOVE each: the new fixture rises on ease_out while the old falls on ease_in_out over twice that, which never sums below the page's whole (at most 23% over, half way).
- **On a board without lit travel** (Light 1's v2-c until its lit bench test), every re-aim blanks the spot for the whole move and its settle. So the aim holds while the page stays within half of half the beam's width (84% of the centre's light). When the page has gone, a standby that has already landed on it takes the job as a crossfade. Only with no standby does the spot fade down, move dark and rise. A real board arrives after its settle (`settle_s`, which the firmware's `predicted_ms` counts), so a dark move relights no sooner.
- **The head and the eyes are checked on the beam as it points.** den-core's eye rule checks the line from the fixture to the page, but a rise in place, the hold without lit travel and a standby's hand-over light a beam pointing a few degrees off that line. So every beam the board emits is checked in every evaluation, whatever den-core said and whether or not a fixture was chosen, on its current aim and at the 12 cm keep margin. The check covers den-core's head point and the eyes (about 3 cm in front of it), where the newest frame saw them and where they're going (their velocity over the frame's age and one more frame). A timed MOVE flying dark is judged at its landing aim, since it emits nothing on the way. A rise in place or a standby's hand-over needs den-core's 15 cm on the beam as it points, or moves dark to the cleared target first.
- **A refusal for the head or the eyes cuts at once**, in one level-0 MOVE, whatever fade the light is in. Easing is only for ordinary hand-offs and exits.
- **The radio rule:** a real fixture is never sent more than 2 commands in any second, keep-lit and Stop counted, and the direction's own commands take at most one of them, so a cut or a Stop never has to be the third (a third is queued behind the others, not refused). Stop, a release (dark at once) and a leaving exit's fade never wait.
- **It leaves gracefully.** Standing up fades out in 0.3 s, dark within 1 s of standing. On a real board it goes dark at once (`leave_fade_board_s` 0): detection and the send path take about 0.9 s, which leaves plan 05's bar C (dark within 1.0 s on 4 of 5) no room for a fade. A task that ends while you're still seated (an answer, the phone patience) fades out over 1 s and is dark at its deadline. A hand-off crossfades. No exit keeps a beam on past the validity a snapshot already promised. Unchanged and still instant: Stop, Off, expiry, a stale or unsafe snapshot, and another person binding. A fixture in the middle of a glide gets one level-0 MOVE to its own target, because the board holds a MOVE that arrives during a move until it lands.
- **The room follows the spot.** The snapshot gains `ambient`. It carries the room glow now (`level`, `scale`, `cct_k`) and the cue it's on (`goal_level`, `goal_scale`, `goal_cct_k`, `phase`, `ramp_s` and `ramp_starts_mono`), so a bulb that fades itself can take one plan per cue. Under a task light, the room's glow dims to 40% (and warms to 2700 K) over 4 s, starting a beat after the spot. It comes back over 1.5 s as the spot leaves. It's published only: nothing is written to Home Assistant, and the real Hue lights are untouched.
- **New settings,** all changeable from Home: `fade_in_s` 2.0, `fade_out_s` 1.0, `leave_fade_s` 0.3 (`leave_fade_board_s` 0 on a real board), `crossfade_s` 1.2, `shift_m` 0.06, `shift_hold_s` 1.5, `page_up_m` 0.05, `facing_keep_deg` 5, and the ambient levels and times.
- **Before and after**, on a synthetic reading session at the camera's live rate, lag and ask pace: sit, open the book (at the chest or in the lap), three page turns, a 35 cm shift along the sofa, stand. 60 runs per case (20 seeds, 3 facings), with percentiles pooled over every lit sample. The page's true place is a family chosen apart from either rule (the centre of plausible grips, and 8 cm off it forward, sideways and up), because the round-3 page sat 3 cm from where the new rule looked. It is a model, not the room.
  - **The aim** is about as close to the page at the median as before (9 cm either way, chest or lap), but the tail is shorter: the pooled p95 goes from 25 to 19 cm at the chest and from 22 to 19 cm in the lap. Within half the beam's width: from 93–95% to 96%. On round 3's own lap page: 4.4 cm, against the old rule's 4.5 cm. A book held in one hand isn't helped (16–25 cm). On a board without lit travel the hold keeps the beam where it was through a 35 cm shift at a far fixture: about 31 cm off the page once the book is still again, within half the beam's width 36–43% of the time, with two thirds of the page's light (with lit travel, 3 cm and 98–100%).
  - **The page's light** (any dip below 70% counts) dips 0.8–1.3 times a session with lit travel (1.3–2.0 before), and 1.1–1.4 without it (1.8–2.8). Most of those are the reader's own 35 cm move: the spot follows once the book is still. Cuts to dark: the head-clearance check catches the reader sliding toward a held beam (0.13–0.2 a session). At the 5° keep margin, the pose's noise also cuts a side-on socket's light about 0.5 times a session in a noisy pose (none at 10°). A one-frame pose dropout still darkens at once, by the rule.
  - **The head:** in fast turns of 45° and 90° while lit, no beam's centre comes within 10 cm of the eyes (closest 12.3 cm on a board without lit travel, 14.7 cm on a virtual fixture; 14.6 cm before).
  - **Yes to first light:** 0.10 s at p50 on a virtual fixture (0.72 s before at the chest), 0.56 s at p95. On the real boards, 0.58 s at p50 and 1.1–1.2 s at p95, worst 3.24 s: the free radio slot makes the rise's MOVE wait out the second after the last dark pre-aim, and in the worst case noise has every side-on socket just in front of the reader when the pre-aim lands. Standing to dark: 0.86 s at p50, at most 1.06 s, on a virtual fixture. On a real board, with plan 05's 0.3 s send path added, 0.88 s at p50 and at most 1.06 s, within 1.0 s on 80% of stand-ups.
